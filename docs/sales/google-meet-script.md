# Google Meet Script — 3–5 Property Pilot Call

> 25–30 minute call. Goal: a clear yes/no on a pilot, not a "great deck." Read
> `ABOUT ME/voice-profile.md` first — peer-to-peer, direct, zero pitch theater.

## 30-second intro
"Thanks for the time — I'll keep this tight. I run Archer Design: property-level creative for hospitality groups — graphics, short-form motion, F&B and event promos, photo polish — as an outside system instead of another hire. I work with Hotel Indigo Pittsburgh, a couple of Hampton properties, and hotel F&B like Eliza Hot Metal Bistro. I pulled a few thoughts on [Company]'s portfolio. Want me to walk through them, or would you rather tell me where creative actually hurts first?"
*(Offering them the floor first is the whole tone of the call.)*

## Setting context (before slide 1)
"Quick frame: this isn't an agency pitch. I'm one senior creative system, fixed fee, built for groups where each property can't justify its own hire. If it's not a fit, I'd rather we figure that out in 25 minutes."

## Presenting each slide
- **Slide 1 (their portfolio):** lead with something genuinely good you found. Then the gap: "Here's where the feeds diverge." Ask: *"Is this centralized, or does each property run its own?"* Then stop talking.
- **Slide 2 (problem):** "Sound familiar, or is your setup different?" Let them correct you — their correction is the discovery.
- **Slide 3 (opportunity):** anchor to *their* metric: rate integrity for luxury, direct bookings for select-service, covers for F&B-heavy.
- **Slide 4 (proof):** show 2–3 before/afters in their category. Don't read stats out loud unless asked — let them sit on the slide.
- **Slide 5 (pilot):** "This is the part that matters. One month, 3–5 properties, everything you see here, fixed fee." Pause. Price plainly: "Three properties starts at $4,500 a month; five at $10,000."
- **Slide 6 (next step):** "If you ran this, which properties would you pick?" — this question converts. They start planning, the decision is half made.

## Questions to ask (sprinkle, don't interrogate)
- "Who touches creative today, per property?"
- "What's the approval chain — corporate, property, or both?"
- "Which property embarrasses you most online?" (said warmly — it gets a laugh and the truth)
- "What's coming in the next 90 days that needs promotion — seasons, events, openings?"

## Avoiding salesy
No superlatives, no urgency, no "imagine if." Cite specifics from their feeds. Say "I don't know" when you don't. Quote prices without flinching or justifying. The premium signal is calm.

## Transition to pilot
"Based on what you've said, the version I'd scope is [3 or 5] properties: [names]. Want me to send the formal pilot proposal with those properties and exact pricing?"

## Asking for next step
"What does the decision path look like on your side — is this yours to call, or do we need [owner/VP] in the loop? Happy to do a 15-minute version of this for them."

## Objection handling
- **"We already have a team."** "Most groups I work with do. The internal team owns strategy and brand; I'm the overflow that keeps property-level output consistent when they're slammed — campaign visuals, F&B promos, motion, seasonal pushes. Extension, not replacement. What does your team wish it had time for?"
- **"Send info."** "Will do — I'll send the pilot one-pager plus two examples matched to [category]. So I send the right version: is the interest more rooms-level creative, F&B, or portfolio consistency?" *(Always attach a question to the send.)*
- **"Budget is tight."** "Understood — that's actually the argument. The loaded cost of one creative hire is $90K+; the 3-property pilot is a fraction of that, monthly, cancellable. And we can start with three properties instead of five. What budget line does creative currently live in?"
- **"We use brand templates."** "Templates cover the flag layer well — logo use, room shots, brand campaigns. Where they're thin is the property layer: your F&B specials, local events, weddings, seasonal pushes. That's exactly the layer I cover, brand-standard-aware so it clears review. Want to see a Hampton example that passed brand review first try?"
