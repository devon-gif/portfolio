# Before/After Proof Library

> One entry per transformation type. Each has: headline, labels, what changed,
> why it matters, LinkedIn caption, website card copy, and the sales-call line.
> Build the actual asset pairs in the Creative Sprint (Day 2, 7, 11, 19 assignments).

## 1. Hotel exterior/photo → premium campaign asset
- **Headline:** Same property. Different perceived value.
- **Before label:** Raw exterior photo, straight off the drive
- **After label:** Polished property campaign asset
- **What changed:** Light, color, crop, branded treatment, campaign framing with a booking reason.
- **Why it matters:** Perceived value per scroll is what guests compare — before they ever compare rates.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "Same property, same photo, one polish pass. The difference is what a guest feels — and books. Most hotels don't need a reshoot; they need finishing."
- **Website card:** Before: a photo in a folder. After: a campaign asset that earns the rate.
- **Sales-call line:** "This took under an hour, no shoot. Now imagine it across your portfolio every week."

## 2. Room/lobby photo → luxury-feeling property creative
- **Headline:** The $130-a-night perception gap.
- **Before:** Flat, under-edited room photo · **After:** Editorial-grade room creative
- **What changed:** Tone curve, warmth, composition crop, restrained typography.
- **Why it matters:** The same room reads $159 or $289 depending on the finish. Creative protects rate.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "Same room. Which one books at the higher rate? Polish isn't cosmetic — it's rate integrity."
- **Website card:** Rooms that look like what they cost.
- **Sales-call line:** "Your ADR says premium. Does the feed agree?"

## 3. Food/menu item → F&B campaign promo
- **Headline:** One phone photo. One campaign asset.
- **Before:** Kitchen phone photo of a dish · **After:** Finished F&B promo with offer framing
- **What changed:** Color/texture work, branded layout, special/day/season hook, caption.
- **Why it matters:** F&B is local revenue rooms never captures — and the most under-promoted asset on property.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "Hotel restaurants are often the most under-marketed asset on property. This started as a phone photo from the line."
- **Website card:** From the pass to the feed, while the special is still on the menu.
- **Sales-call line:** "Your chef already shoots this on a phone. We finish it before the special changes."

## 4. Event details → hotel booking campaign
- **Headline:** A local event is a booking reason.
- **Before:** An event happening near the property, unmentioned · **After:** Event-tied stay campaign (rooms + event + F&B)
- **What changed:** The event becomes the creative hook; the hotel becomes the obvious place to stay.
- **Why it matters:** Demand spikes have a booking window; creative that ships inside it captures bookings competitors miss.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "In town for [event]? That sentence is a campaign. Most hotels post about the event after it sells out."
- **Website card:** Every local event, turned into a reason to book your property.
- **Sales-call line:** "What's the biggest event near [property] in the next 90 days? That's pilot month one."

## 5. Plain event schedule → branded monthly calendar
- **Headline:** Nobody screenshots a paragraph.
- **Before:** Text list of the month's events · **After:** Branded calendar guests save and share
- **What changed:** Designed, reusable calendar system in the property's brand.
- **Why it matters:** One asset promotes every event of the month and lives on phones, not in a feed's past.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "Turned a text schedule into the most-saved asset this property posts. Format > volume."
- **Website card:** The whole month's programming, in one asset worth saving.
- **Sales-call line:** "This one gets reused twelve times a year. It pays for its design in month one."

## 6. Spa service → wellness campaign
- **Headline:** Creative as calm as the treatment room.
- **Before:** Treatment list on a webpage · **After:** Serene, premium wellness campaign creative
- **What changed:** A quiet visual language, service-specific assets, seasonal timing (gift seasons especially).
- **Why it matters:** The spa is a margin line that books on feeling; creative that matches the room sells the room.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "Spas sell calm. The creative has to feel like the treatment room — this is what that looks like."
- **Website card:** Wellness creative that finally matches the experience.
- **Sales-call line:** "Gift-card season is a revenue spike most hotel spas meet with a text post."

## 7. Raw phone photo → polished premium visual
- **Headline:** The phone photo was fine. The finish was missing.
- **Before:** Staff phone snap · **After:** Polished, on-brand visual
- **What changed:** Professional retouch + brand treatment — proof the system works without a photographer.
- **Why it matters:** Kills the "we need a photo shoot first" objection; content can start this week.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "Shot on a phone by the front desk team. Finished by the system. You don't have a photo problem."
- **Website card:** Start with what your team already shoots. We handle the finish.
- **Sales-call line:** "Your team already creates the raw material daily. Nothing reaches the feed finished — that's the whole gap."

## 8. Hotel group portfolio → consistency system
- **Headline:** 20 properties. One brand. Or 20 small hotels.
- **Before:** Grid of mismatched property feeds · **After:** Unified portfolio with property-level personality
- **What changed:** New branded creative built as a system — templates, motion language, rules — not one-off graphics.
- **Why it matters:** Portfolio coherence is a corporate asset: owner relations, brand audits, the next flag deal.
- **LinkedIn caption:** "If every property posts differently, the portfolio feels smaller than it is. Consistency is a growth asset."
- **Website card:** Group-level consistency, property-level personality, one system.
- **Sales-call line:** "Pull up your five properties' feeds side by side. Would a stranger know they're related?"
