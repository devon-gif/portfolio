## What I do

I turn a hospitality property's *existing* visual assets — property photos, F&B and event content, seasonal campaigns, menus, and brand files — into polished, ready-to-post creative. That includes:

- Social graphics (Instagram, Facebook, stories, carousels)
- Short-form motion content (Reels, TikTok, vertical video)
- Event and wedding promos
- Restaurant / F&B visuals (menu features, specials, seasonal pushes)
- Spa and wellness campaign creative
- Branded marketing assets (promos, local partnerships, seasonal launches)

I work as an outside creative partner, not an in-house hire. Teams send me what they already have; I send back finished, on-brand content on a recurring basis.

## Best-fit customers

- They already have decent raw photos/video but lack the time or skill to turn it into finished content.
- The marketing or social workload sits on a GM, AGM, sales manager, or one overworked marketing person.
- They post inconsistently, recycle the same few images, or go quiet for weeks.
- They are considering (or dreading) hiring an in-house designer / social person / videographer.
- They value speed and reliability over endless revisions.
- They have a brand standard to protect (flag brands especially).
- Multi-property operators who need consistency across locations without consistent headcount.

---

## Bad-fit customers

- Pre-opening properties with no photos, no brand assets, and no content to work from yet.
- Teams that want a one-time logo or a single flyer and nothing ongoing.
- Brands that want full original photo/video *shoots* as the core deliverable (I work from existing assets first).
- Companies looking for the absolute cheapest freelancer with no quality bar.
- Buyers who need paid-media management / ad buying as the main ask (that's not my core service).
- Anyone who wants me to manage their accounts and write all copy strategy end-to-end with no asset input.

## Tone rules

- Sound like a real person who knows hospitality, not a marketer running a script.
- Casual, warm, confident, low-pressure. Peer-to-peer, not vendor-to-prospect.
- Short. Say less. One clear idea per message.
- Specific to *their* property — reference something real I actually saw.
- Curious, not pitchy. Lead with an observation or a genuine compliment, not an ask.
- Never desperate. I have clients; I'm offering capacity, not begging for work.
- Plain language. No jargon, no buzzwords, no corporate filler.

## Rules for sounding human and not like AI

1. **Reference one real, specific detail** from their site/page/post. If I can't, I don't send yet.
2. **Lead with an observation or compliment**, not an ask.
3. **One idea per message.** No stacked value props.
4. **Contractions and casual punctuation.** "you've," "I'd," dashes, lowercase starts are fine.
5. **No template skeleton.** Vary sentence length and openings every time.
6. **Cut the throat-clearing.** Delete "I wanted to reach out," "I hope this finds you well."
7. **Show, don't claim.** Offer the free 5 pieces instead of describing how good I am.
8. **Sound busy, not needy.** I'm offering bandwidth, not asking for a favor.
9. **Banned word check** every time (see "Phrases I should avoid").
10. **Read it out loud test.** If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
11. **Match their register.** A GM gets casual; a corporate brand director gets a touch more polished — still human.
12. **End low-pressure.** "No worries if not the right time" beats "Looking forward to your response!"
